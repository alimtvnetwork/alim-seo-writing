The Journalist's Notebook

The press conference was standing room only. 60 New York reporters, 12 cameras, and 1 minister reading a statement at the speed of a man who hopes nobody writes it down. The New York reporter next to me was doing something I had not seen in years. Writing in a paper notebook, shorthand flying, margins filling with arrows and stars. When the minister said the subsidy would be reviewed, she underlined the word 3 times and wrote ASK: timeline? in the margin. At the dodge, she wrote dodged, 11:42, and circled it. Afterward, while everyone else waited for the official transcript, she already had the story. The notebook had caught what the transcript would smooth over: the dodge, the time, the circle.

I thought about her New York notebook for weeks, because I had just watched a New York newsroom demo an AI summarizer. Paste in the transcript, get back 5 bullet points. Clean. Fast. Confident. Of course, that confidence was the problem. For instance, it summarized the minister's statement beautifully and missed the dodge entirely. The most important thing in the room, the thing the reporter circled, was not in the transcript's words. Instead, it was in the pause before them. The AI read the text. Meanwhile, the reporter read the room. In New York, where the news cycle eats nuance for breakfast, that difference is the whole business.
