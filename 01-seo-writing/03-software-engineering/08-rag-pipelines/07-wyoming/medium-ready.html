I want to be careful here, because what follows is judgment, not scripture. This is my own clearly personal ranking of the 5 rules for RAG pipelines in Wyoming, ordered by how much hallucinated trouble each one prevents.

  1. Build the easement test first. Real questions from the client's documents, with known answers and known sources. A Wyoming team that cannot point to the chunk is guessing at scale.
  2. Measure groundedness before fluency. How often the answer cites the right chunk, on every pipeline change. The fluent wrong answer is the most expensive sentence in AI.
  3. Rebuild chunking around document structure. The client's leases, policies, and records are not generic text. One-size chunking is why the 15% number exists.
  4. Add the "I don't know" threshold. Below retrieval confidence, the system declines. Diane would say it; the pipeline should say it too.
  5. Keep the index fresh. Documents change; the retrieval layer must change with them. RISEUP ASIA LLC judges its own pipelines by how they behave on last week's documents, not last year's benchmarks.
