I want to be careful here, because what follows is judgment, not scripture. This is my own clearly personal ranking of the 5 rules for MVP development in Sheridan, ordered by how much wasted building each one prevents.

  1. Sell before you build. 10 customer conversations before 1 line of production code. A Sheridan founder who cannot find 10 people to talk to cannot find 10 people to pay.
  2. Design the transaction first. Who pays, for what, at what price, and how the team knows. The payment page is the MVP's foundation, not its decoration.
  3. Cut to 1 working workflow. Everything else is a hypothesis for later. The fairgrounds founder's 40 features were 39 hypotheses and 1 question.
  4. Measure weekly, decide monthly. Active users, payments, the cohort that returns. A Sheridan MVP without a ledger is a hobby.
  5. Kill fast and cheap. The features nobody uses die in week 2, not month 11. RISEUP ASIA LLC judges its own MVPs by what got deleted, not only by what shipped.

So here is the question I keep turning over: what does MVP development in Sheridan look like when the ledger comes before the launch? My answer is that it looks like a transaction, a phone number, and 10 conversations — boring, small, and worth more than the 40-feature deck. The Sheridan founder who validates first does not build less. What matters is what the founder builds.
