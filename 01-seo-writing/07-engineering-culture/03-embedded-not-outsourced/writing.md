# The In-N-Out Line: Why California Joins the Team Instead of Outsourcing It

The In-N-Out line in Baldwin Park, California stretched past the palm trees at 12:15, and I had 40 minutes to watch the kitchen work. New crew members did not stand off to the side with a manual. Rather, they stepped onto the line, took a station, and started cooking while a veteran watched their hands. Within an hour the new hire was not a vendor of burgers. Instead, the new hire was a cook. California understands this instinctively. You do not outsource the line; you join it.

Every handoff costs context: the vendor's engineer relearns the domain each sprint, the client's team re-explains the roadmap each quarter, and the knowledge walks out when the contract ends. As a result, most California founders I meet have paid for the same system twice, once to the vendor and once to the team that rebuilt it.
