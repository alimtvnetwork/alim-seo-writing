These are the 5 retrieval lessons from librarians, most valuable first. The criterion comes straight from Alim's evaluation playbook, and Boston's librarians would approve of every item.

  1. "I don't know" is a feature. The bravest sentence in librarianship — Boston's librarians say it daily — is also the bravest in AI. A system that abstains when nothing is found is trustworthy; a system that invents is a liability. Measure and publish the abstention rate.
  2. The index is the product. Chunking, metadata, and curation determine answer quality more than model choice. Boston's great catalogs prove this. A mediocre model over a superb index beats a superb model over garbage — every time, measurably.
  3. Evaluate with real questions. Boston's researchers would insist: build a test set of actual user questions with known-good answers from the documents, and grade every change against it. Vibes-based evaluation ("looks good to me") is how fictional citations ship.
  4. Cite or stay silent. Every claim the system makes should trace to a retrievable passage. Boston's academic standards demand nothing less. If the pipeline cannot produce the source, the answer does not ship. Citations are not decoration; they are the audit trail.
  5. Weed the collection. Boston's librarians discard outdated books; indexes need the same discipline. Stale documents retrieved confidently are worse than no documents at all. Curation is ongoing, unglamorous, and non-negotiable.
