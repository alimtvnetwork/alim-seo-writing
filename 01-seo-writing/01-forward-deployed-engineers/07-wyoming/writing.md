I took a booth near the window and watched Wyoming wake up through the glass: ranch trucks, a snowplow with its blade up, a pickup with a cracked windshield and a bumper sticker about minding your own business.

2 tables over, a man in a Carhartt jacket was on a video call with someone in an office somewhere far from Wyoming. Voices carry in a truck stop at dawn, and this one carried frustration. The dispatch software had crashed again at 4 AM, the caller explained, and 14 trucks were sitting idle in a yard outside Laramie because the routing screen would not load. On the other end of the line, someone kept saying the word "ticket." The man kept saying the word "trucks."

By the time my eggs arrived, the call had ended the way those calls always end. Predictably, nothing was fixed. A ticket had been created. Meanwhile, somewhere far from Wyoming, a queue had grown by 1, and 14 trucks in Wyoming were still sitting in the cold. The man paid for the coffee, pulled the jacket tight, and walked out to deal with it.
