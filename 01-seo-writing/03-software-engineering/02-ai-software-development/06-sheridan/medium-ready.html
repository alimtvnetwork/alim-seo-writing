I want to be careful here, because what follows is judgment, not scripture. This is my own clearly personal ranking of the 5 rules that separate AI software that ships from demos that stall in Sheridan, scored on 1 criterion: which rules decide whether a Sheridan customer ever notices the AI working.

  1. Define the success metric before choosing the model. Accuracy, correction rate, or dollars saved, written down in advance. A Sheridan team that cannot define done cannot recognize it, and the vendor who resists writing it down is telling you something.

  2. Build the evaluation harness second. Real Sheridan questions with known answers, run on every change. Unevaluated AI is a rumor mill with an API, and the rumor always sounds confident.

  3. Design the failure path before the happy path. Decide what the system does when the model is wrong, slow, or down. The whiteboard needs the "wrong answers" box, because a Sheridan production incident will draw it anyway.

  4. Measure cost per transaction from day 1. Tokens, latency, and the invoice, tracked like any other budget line. A clever Sheridan feature at $4 per transaction is a liability wearing a costume.

  5. Keep a human in the loop for the irreversible. Approvals for money, records, and anything that cannot be undone. RISEUP ASIA LLC judges its own AI systems by what happens when they are wrong, not only when they are right.
