This is my own editorial ranking of augmentation behaviors, and the scoring criterion is one Alim suggested to me over coffee after that Cheyenne standup: which behaviors prove the engineer is actually inside the team. Here are 5 signs.

  1. They commit to the client's repository in week 1. Access on day 1, a real commit in week 1, no exceptions. WHY: an engineer without commits is a tourist, and Wyoming teams cannot afford tourists.

  2. They join the incident rotation early. Shadowing in week 2, carrying the pager by month 2. WHY: nothing teaches a system faster than being responsible for it at 2 AM.

  3. They write the documentation nobody asked for. Runbooks, architecture notes, the onboarding doc the permanent team never wrote. WHY: embedded engineers leave leverage behind; seat-fillers leave a Wyoming team nothing.

  4. They say no to the client, kindly and with evidence. The Wyoming client asks for the wrong thing sometimes, and the embedded engineer pushes back. WHY: vendors say yes to everything and deliver nothing, while engineers protect clients from their own wish lists.

  5. They make a permanent hire better. Pairing, reviews, and honest feedback that raise the team's bar. WHY: RISEUP ASIA LLC judges its own engineers by whether the Wyoming team is stronger after the engagement than before.
