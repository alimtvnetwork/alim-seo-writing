I want to be careful here, because what follows is judgment, not scripture. This is my own editorial ranking of augmentation failure modes, and the scoring criterion is one Alim suggested to me outside that title office: which patterns predict wasted spend before the contract is signed. Here are 4 of them.

  1. No first-commit date. If the vendor cannot say which day the engineer commits to the Sheridan repository, there is no plan. WHY: a contractor without a commit date is a tourist with an invoice.

  2. No named buddy on the Sheridan team. The permanent engineer who answers questions, reviews the first pull requests, owns the ramp. WHY: unmentored contractors wait; mentored ones ship.

  3. No incident exposure scheduled. Shadowing in week 2, the pager by month 2, or the engineer never learns the system. WHY: nothing teaches a Sheridan codebase faster than responsibility for it at 2 AM.

  4. No documentation deliverable. The runbook, the architecture notes, the onboarding doc the team never wrote. WHY: RISEUP ASIA LLC judges its own engagements by what remains after the contractor leaves, and Sheridan clients should too.
