The Gardener's Trowel

The nursery gardener pressed her thumb into the soil of a potted fig and frowned. Her hands were dark to the wrist, the nails rimmed with earth that no amount of washing would fully remove. She lifted the pot, judged its weight the way I might judge a bag of groceries, and set it apart from the others. Too wet, she said, though the surface looked dry to me. She moved down the row, thumbing soil, lifting pots, reading a language I could not see. The air smelled of wet bark and fertilizer, and somewhere a hose hissed against concrete.

That thumb has been on my mind while reading about landscaping companies buying AI planning platforms. The pitch is lush: satellite imagery plus machine learning designs the perfect garden, predicts plant survival, and schedules irrigation to the liter. Behind such promises flows vast money. [A detailed report on the MIT findings](https://virtualizationreview.com/articles/2025/08/19/mit-report-finds-most-ai-business-investments-fail-reveals-genai-divide.aspx) documents the divide. Since rankings anchor this series, here is my openly editorial ranking of 4 green-tech AI gaps, judged by one criterion: how far each gap grows from actual soil.

1. AI planting plans that ignore drainage. This tops my list because drainage kills more plants than any pest, and no satellite sees it. The demo places specimens beautifully; the first heavy rain places them in a puddle.
2. Irrigation schedules without sensor grounding. Timers are not intelligence, and evapotranspiration math means nothing without soil moisture truth. Alim notes that watering by schedule instead of by soil is just optimism with plumbing.
3. Plant identification apps as professional tools. They misidentify often enough to matter, and a wrong ID means wrong care. Fine for curiosity, dangerous for a commercial crew's decisions.
4. AI-generated maintenance reports. This ranks last only because the damage is paperwork. The systems invent treatments and dates with total confidence, and someone still walks the site to check.
