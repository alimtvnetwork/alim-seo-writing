# Infographic Philosophy — The Teacher's Red Pen

The story defends the teacher's judgment against automation that grades the surface. The infographics quantify the grading gap and the alternative.

## Infographic 1 — the stat card (16.1)

The largest average gap between AI and human essay marks — 16.1, with 40 at the individual level — is the story's hardest evidence that machine grading is a different exam. The number is set large and the academic source (Kay et al., 2026) is credited, because in education the citation is part of the credibility.

## Infographic 2 — the compare card

Red column (THE AI-SPENDING PATTERN) versus green column (THE ENGINEER-LED PATTERN): surface grading vs rubric grounding, compression vs human benchmarking, silent scale failure vs batch monitoring, deference vs designed-in sign-off. Each row restates one of the story's 4 findings as a choice, keeping the teacher-in-the-loop answer visible.

## Infographic 3 — the ranking card

The 4 things AI tutors get wrong, headed by the stated criterion (damage to learning). The WHY lines are short moral summaries — "teaches safe writing", "deference becomes policy" — so the card reads as a warning label, which is exactly its function in the article.
