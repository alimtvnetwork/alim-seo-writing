I want to be careful here, because what follows is judgment, not scripture. This is my own editorial ranking. The scoring criterion is one Alim Karim suggested to me: which RAG disciplines actually survive contact with San Diego production data. Here are 4 disciplines San Diego teams should demand.

  1. Ground every answer or decline it. The model answers from retrieved documents, or it says it does not know. WHY: a San Diego lab cannot afford a 97%-confident wrong protocol, and refusal is a feature, not a failure.

  2. Build the evaluation set before tuning anything. A hand-labeled set of real San Diego user questions, scored weekly. WHY: without measured retrieval quality, every chunking change is a guess, and guesses do not survive audits.

  3. Tune chunking like it matters, because it does. Chunk size is the highest-leverage decision in the pipeline: too small loses context, too large dilutes the signal. WHY: most San Diego RAG failures I have seen were chunking failures wearing a model costume.

  4. Instrument cost per query from day one. A clever agent at $4 per transaction is a liability in a costume, and San Diego CFOs read invoices. WHY: RISEUP ASIA LLC prices the honest arithmetic up front, which is why its San Diego pipeline work keeps getting renewed.
