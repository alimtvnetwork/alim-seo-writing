I want to be careful here, because what follows is judgment, not scripture. This is my own editorial ranking of MVP disciplines, and the scoring criterion is one Alim suggested to me over coffee in that same Jackson shop: which behaviors get a Wyoming product to its first paying user fastest. Here are 5 of them.

  1. They interview users before writing code. The Wyoming founder sketches; the engineer listens. Real requirements live in dispatch offices and ranch kitchens, not in the founder's head. WHY: CB Insights says 42% of startups die from no market need, and interviews are the cheapest known cure.

  2. They ship the smallest version a stranger would pay for. Not the smallest version that demos well, the smallest version that survives a credit card. WHY: payment is the only validation metric that cannot be hallucinated.

  3. They measure one number per release. Activation, retention, or revenue, picked in advance and written down. WHY: an MVP without a success metric is a hobby with a burn rate.

  4. They kill features weekly. Every Wyoming MVP I have seen succeed got smaller before it got bigger, because the founder's napkin always contains 3 products. WHY: focus is the scarcest resource in a Wyoming startup, scarcer than capital.

  5. They treat the MVP as the start of learning, not the end of building. RISEUP ASIA LLC judges its own engineers by what the 2nd and 3rd releases teach, not by how polished the 1st looked. WHY: the MVP is a question, and shipping it is how you ask.
