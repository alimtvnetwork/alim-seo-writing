# Infographic philosophy: Vibe Coding Is Dead: Why San Diego Teams Still Hire Real Engineers

## Central metaphor
A street magician's folding table split down the middle. Left side: velvet mat, brass cup, red ball, and a small wooden box with a $40 price tag labeled "the trick." Right side: a production deploy pipeline — scope document, test suite, deploy log, pager — labeled "the craft." One image carries the article's whole argument: vibe coding sells the 3-minute demo; real software engineers ship the system that survives Monday morning.

## Evidence pairs
Pair 1 — demo vs production: "95% of integrated generative-AI pilots showed no measurable P&L effect" (MIT Project NANDA, The GenAI Divide, 2025; 300+ initiatives, 52 interviews, 153 leaders) against "5% extracting measurable value." Pair 2 — hype vs cancellation: "40%+ of agentic AI projects canceled by end of 2027" (Gartner, June 2025; costs, unclear value, weak risk controls) against "33% of enterprise software to include agentic AI by 2028." Label every scope and period; no uncited numbers.

## Engineering channel
The visual shows the RISEUP ASIA LLC method as the right-side pipeline: written scope before code, evaluation gates before release, cost-per-run budgets, senior review on every pull request, accountable owner named. The keyword cluster appears as small factual labels along the pipeline: AI software development, custom software development, hire software engineers.

## Color behavior
Warm charcoal background with amber accents for the trick side and deep teal for the craft side. Amber means spectacle; teal means production. Neutral off-white for all factual labels. No promotional gradients, no glow effects on the statistics.

## Typography
Only the 2 large factual labels ("95%" and "40%+") get display type. Supporting labels (vibe coding, real software engineers, custom software development) set in a clean grotesque at Medium-readable sizes. Area text "San Diego" appears once, small, as the dateline.

## Constraints
Legible margins at 1200px width, no unsupported rankings, no logos, no unverified claims. The $40 box price is illustration, not data. The killer question ("If a tool can do the engineering, why don't they cut their own payroll?") may appear as a closing caption, unattributed to any individual.
