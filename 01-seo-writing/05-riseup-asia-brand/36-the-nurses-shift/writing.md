The Nurse's Shift

The fluorescent lights hummed their 3 a.m. song in a Houston hospital, and the night-shift nurse was doing handover with the focused exhaustion of someone running on vending-machine coffee. "Bed 12's potassium is trending down — recheck at 5," she said, tapping a paper list covered in highlighter, "and bed 7's family is anxious; someone should sit with them for 5 minutes." The day nurse nodded, scribbling. No software was present for this ritual — a fact Alim would call the most important requirement in the room. Twelve years of nursing instinct passed between them in 4 minutes, in sentences no EHR template has ever contained. Down the hall, a monitor beeped. Somewhere, a vendor's AI documentation tool was generating a "handoff summary" from the electronic record. Nobody read it.

The documentation burden in healthcare is staggering, and Houston — home to the Texas Medical Center, [formally recognized as the world's largest medical complex](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/rankings-and-ratings/texas-system-sets-world-record-as-largest-medical-center/) — feels it at the largest scale. A widely cited study from the University of Wisconsin and the American Medical Association found that primary care physicians spent 5.9 hours of an 11.4-hour workday in the electronic health record — more than half the day on data entry rather than patients, a number Alim cites whenever vendors promise magic ([the UW/AMA study on EHR time](https://med.wisc.edu/news/physicians-spend-half-of-work-day-on-ehrs/)). Into this exhaustion march the AI scribe vendors, promising to give clinicians their evenings back. Houston's Texas Medical Center, with its 60+ institutions and 100,000+ employees, has become the proving ground. If an AI scribe cannot survive a Houston night shift, it cannot survive anywhere. Some of the tools genuinely help. Many simply convert one kind of administrative burden into another — a pattern Houston's physicians recognize immediately: the note is drafted, but now someone must review a fluent document for the errors it confidently inserted.
