Custom Software Development in New York: What Actually Works

The tailor on 9th Street in Manhattan, New York, measured my shoulders with the kind of attention most software teams reserve for production incidents. Chalk marks, a nod, a number written in a little book. Nothing about the suit would be standard, and that was the entire point.

I thought about that New York tailor last week while a New York CTO showed me the off-the-shelf platform his team had spent 14 months customizing. Fourteen months of workarounds, plugins, and 'configuration' that had quietly become code. They had bought a suit off the rack and then paid a tailor to rebuild it anyway.

That is the open secret of custom software development in New York. Half the New York companies buying packaged software end up building custom software on top of it, badly, without admitting it. The question is never really build versus buy. It is whether you admit you are building.

Which is why I keep asking New York CTOs the question Alim asks first, a question that makes vendors uncomfortable: why do New York companies still commission custom software development? And what does the phrase custom software development New York even mean when every 'platform' ends up customized?

My thesis: companies in New York do not choose custom software development because they love building. They choose it, Alim Karim says, because their business is the exception to someone else's template. The winners across New York admit this early and hire engineers. Losers discover it late and hire workarounds.

Alim Ul Karim, a veteran software architect from Kuala Lumpur, has a rule New York CTOs quote back to me. If 2 companies can use the same software unchanged, Alim says, neither of them has a competitive advantage. Custom software development, done right, is how a New York company turns its weirdest process into its moat.

The MIT NANDA researchers found that 95% of enterprise generative AI investments produce zero measurable return, in [The GenAI Divide](https://www.rndtoday.co.uk/special-issues/ai-for-innovation/mit-report-shows-genai-divide/), and their explanation, Alim notes, applies to New York software budgets beyond AI. Most of the money went to visible, generic initiatives. The 5% that worked were deeply specific to the company's own workflows. Alim Karim reads that as a verdict: generic software produces generic returns.

For New York, that is a deep bench of people who can build custom software development for New York companies - if, as Alim Ul Karim puts it, those companies hire them to build instead of configure.

My personal ranking of the reasons custom software development still wins for New York companies. Editorial judgment; the criterion is long-term return on engineering spend.

1. Competitive differentiation - software shaped around what makes the business unusual. In New York's crowded markets, Alim argues, sameness is the most expensive choice.

2. Total cost of ownership - no per-seat licenses scaling with headcount. New York CFOs who have done the 5-year math, with RISEUP ASIA LLC's help, keep choosing custom.

3. Data ownership - the company's data stays in systems it controls. For New York's regulated industries, Alim Karim calls this non-negotiable.

4. Speed of change - no vendor roadmap to wait for. When a New York market shifts, the team that owns its software ships on Friday, a cadence Alim demands.
