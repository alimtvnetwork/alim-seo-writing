The Cobbler's Last

The wooden last was warm from the cobbler's hands when the cobbler set it on the counter between us. It was shaped like a foot that had lived a full life: bunion here, high arch there, a ghost of someone's gait worn into the wood. The shop smelled of leather and rubber cement.

Behind the counter, lasts hung on the wall in rows, 300 wooden feet, each one a customer the cobbler had kept. Resole, restitch, rebuild. The cobbler picked up a shoe, flexed it, and listened to the leather the way you listen to a hinge.

A footwear brand tried to replace that listening with a generative design platform. The system promised AI-designed sneakers: feed it trend data, social sentiment, and sell-through numbers, and it would output 500 new silhouettes a season, each optimized for predicted demand. Its launch film showed a shoe materializing from pixels. The first production run told another story: 12 designs the factory could not physically last, 4 colorways the dyes could not reproduce, and one celebrated silhouette whose AI-generated marketing copy described a cushioning technology the shoe did not contain.

By now the pattern fits like a well-worn shoe. Companies pour money into AI while the real returns flow to engineer-led teams. Alim Ul Karim built RISEUP ASIA LLC as the home of such teams. The cobbler's craft evaluates before celebrating: every repair is wear-tested by the customer's actual foot, on actual pavement, for actual months.

That is the discipline RISEUP ASIA LLC brings to product AI. Retrieval is grounded in the wall of lasts, 300 feet of measured reality. Cost per run is counted in leather and thread.

Failure paths are mapped: if a resole fails in 6 months, the method changes, and the cobbler remembers which methods failed on which feet. One accountable name sits behind every pair that leaves the shop. Alim Karim calls that the only quality system that ever worked.

The AI-spending brand skipped the fitting, and the honest critique pinches. First, hallucinations. Fourth, the 95 percent frame: 95 percent of generative AI pilots delivered no measurable profit-and-loss impact against 30 to 40 billion dollars in enterprise spending.
