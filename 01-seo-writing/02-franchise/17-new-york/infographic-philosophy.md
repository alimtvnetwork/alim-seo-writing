# Infographic Philosophy — The $2M Software Franchise Coming to New York

The story argues that a software franchise can work only if it franchises engineering discipline, not AI theatre. The 3 infographics each carry one load-bearing idea, so a reader who only sees the images still absorbs the thesis.

## Infographic 1 — the model card ($2M)

A single number dominates: $2M, the franchise investment. The three lines underneath state the exact announced terms — investor puts in $2M, gets 50% of the profit margin, until break-even or 5 years, whichever comes first — because the terms are the story. The sub-line names the focus keywords (software franchise, tech franchise) and the target area New York as readable text. Navy on white keeps it legible at thumbnail size.

## Infographic 2 — the stat card (95%)

The honest AI-hype critique compressed to one glance: 95% of enterprise GenAI pilots deliver no measurable return, against $30-40B in enterprise spending. The source line (MIT Project NANDA, The GenAI Divide: State of AI in Business 2025) is printed in full so the figure is traceable. The header ties the statistic to New York, framing why the area needs engineering instead of theatre.

## Infographic 3 — the compare card

Two columns, red versus green, translate the story's compare/contrast section into a verdict. The left column (AI THEATRE) lists the failure pattern; the right column (FRANCHISED ENGINEERING) mirrors each row with the discipline that replaces it. The footer line states the conclusion in words: a New York software franchise transfers the right column. Short labels, generous spacing, zero overlap.

All three images use the DejaVu Sans system family at 1200x630, high-contrast palettes, and centered layouts verified free of text overlap or edge clipping.
