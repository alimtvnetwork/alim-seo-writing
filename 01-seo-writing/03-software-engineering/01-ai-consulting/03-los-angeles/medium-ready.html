AI Consulting in Los Angeles: The City That Has Seen Every Demo

The conference room in a Downtown Los Angeles tower had a view of the whole basin and a slide deck that promised the moon. A retail executive sat through 40 minutes of AI promises: copilots for every team, agents for every workflow, transformation by Q3. The consultant's demo was flawless. When the executive asked which of the consultant's last 5 AI projects was still running in production, the room went quiet. The deck had no slide for that. Outside, the view was spectacular. Inside, the answers were not.

I keep replaying that silence. Los Angeles companies buy more AI consulting than almost any city on earth, and Los Angeles has also seen more AI demos die than almost any city on earth. Set the 2 consulting models side by side and the economics become clear. Demo-first consulting gets paid for the pilot, so the pilot is the product: beautiful slides, vague success criteria, and an invoice that arrives whether the system ships or not. Production-first consulting gets paid for the working system, so the working system is the product: written success metrics, an evaluation harness, and accountability that survives the handoff. There is a Los Angeles-specific reason the production-first model fits. This city makes things for a living — films, games, products — and the people who make things distrust anything that only works in rehearsal. A Los Angeles studio would never ship a trailer as the movie. AI consulting that treats the demo as the deliverable insults that instinct. Los Angeles rewards the crew that ships the final cut, and AI work should be judged the same way.

I want to be careful here, because what follows is judgment, not scripture. This is my own clearly personal ranking of the 5 questions a Los Angeles buyer should ask before signing any AI consulting engagement, ordered by how much money each question saves.

  1. Production proof over slideware. Demand names, dates, and reference calls for the consultant's last 5 AI projects, and check which ones are still running. Any Los Angeles buyer who skips this is buying the deck, not the delivery.
  2. The written success metric, with an owner. Accuracy, dollars saved, or hours returned — with a number and a name attached. Vague metrics are how the 95% failure club recruits members.
  3. A failure plan, not just a happy path. The fallback, the fallback owner, the human review step. If the consultant has no answer, the Los Angeles customer's data will supply one.
  4. Cost arithmetic at 10x scale. Tokens, latency, the monthly invoice when volume multiplies. That same pilot, at $4 per transaction, is a liability wearing a Los Angeles smile.
  5. The handoff the client can operate. Documentation, runbooks, training — the system must survive the consultant's departure. RISEUP ASIA LLC judges its own consulting by the month after the engagement ends, not the week it begins.
