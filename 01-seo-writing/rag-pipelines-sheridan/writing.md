This is my own editorial ranking of retrieval failure modes, and the scoring criterion is one Alim suggested to me at that reference desk: which failure modes a Sheridan team must rehearse before trusting a pipeline. Here are 5 of them.

1. Stale documents. The policy updated in March, the index still serving January. WHY: yesterday's truth is today's hallucination, and Sheridan regulators notice.

2. The confident invention. Retrieval finds nothing, the model answers anyway, fluently. WHY: Ruth says "I don't know"; the pipeline must too.

3. The split clause. Chunking severs a contract clause from its exceptions, and the answer quotes half the truth. WHY: retrieval can only find what chunking preserved.

4. Wrong drawers. The query matches an archived version, and the system cites it proudly. WHY: recency and versioning belong in the index, not in hope.
