The Night Market Ledger

Rain threatened the pasar malam on Jalan Alor, and the air smelled of grilled satay, hot oil, and wet concrete. Under a blue tarpaulin, a kuih vendor in her 60s worked a paper notebook with a pencil stub. Every sale got a tick; every unsold tray at closing got a number. She flipped back through weeks of pages, rain spotting the paper, and muttered to her daughter about ordering less coconut for Thursday. No dashboard, no model. Just a ledger, a pencil, and 20 years of Thursdays in Kuala Lumpur.

For instance, I bought 6 pieces of kuih lapis and asked how she knew how much to make. She tapped the notebook. Thursday rain means office workers stay home, she said. Friday payday means double. School holidays mean children, children mean extra. Her forecast was local, specific, grounded in 20 years of observations nobody else had written down. Still, it was more accurate than most retail AI solutions I have seen pitched in Kuala Lumpur.
