Here is the line that divides staff augmentation in Wyoming that helps from augmentation that just adds a dark square to the call. The answer divides on 1 line: whether capability stays when the contractor leaves. Good augmentation transfers knowledge into the client's team. Bad augmentation rents hands, bills hours, and walks out the door with everything learned. I want to be careful here, because what follows is judgment, not scripture. This is my own clearly personal ranking of the 5 rules for staff augmentation in Wyoming, ordered by how much chair-rental theater each one prevents.

  1. Mandate the transfer. The contract should name what the client's team will be able to do alone by month 6. A Wyoming engagement without a transfer clause is a dependency clause.
  2. Interview the engineers, not the vendor. The client's team should meet the actual people. Polished CVs and skipped interviews are the body shop's tell.
  3. Fix day 1 before day 3. Access, laptop, buddy, first task — all ready before the contractor arrives. The dark square is a process failure, not a people failure.
  4. Measure documentation from week 1. Every module gets an owner and a doc. The module nobody owns is the Cheyenne meeting room's epitaph.
  5. Plan the exit at the start. The best augmented engineer works toward their own departure. RISEUP ASIA LLC judges its own engagements by what stays after the contract ends.
