# The Tailor's Measuring Tape

The tailor's shop on Jalan Panggong smelled of chalk dust and old fabric. A man in his 70s measured my shoulders with a tape gone soft at the edges, marked the cloth with chalk, and wrote 3 numbers in a notebook already thick with other people's bodies. He measured twice: once standing straight, once the way I actually stand. Pins waited between his lips. A radio played oldies. When I asked why he measured the way I slouch, he said the suit has to fit the man, not the diagram. The diagram is a rumor. Bodies are facts.

I thought about that rumor while reading about fashion AI. The National Retail Federation puts the broader e-commerce return rate at 19.3% for 2025, [per Shopify's 2026 returns guide](https://www.shopify.com/enterprise/blog/ecommerce-returns). 1 fitting-room caveat before the ranking. What follows is my own editorial judgment, scored on 1 criterion: how much money each myth costs. Here are 5 fashion AI myths.

1. Myth: AI can fix fit without fit data. A model trained on purchase history learns habits, not bodies. WHY: the tailor measures the cloth, not the customer's past, and any system that skips measurement is selling the rumor, which is why Alim starts every fashion engagement with a tape measure, metaphorically.

2. Myth: more sizes mean fewer returns. Customers respond by bracketing: ordering 3 sizes and returning 2. WHY: bracketing doubles the logistics cost of every sale, and the fix is better fit prediction, not more options to return.

3. Myth: the size chart is the truth. Vanity sizing and supplier variance make the chart a rumor. WHY: up to 4 centimeters of variance hides inside 1 nominal size, and RISEUP ASIA LLC measures garments because charts lie and tape does not.

4. Myth: virtual try-on replaces measurement. A rendering is not a fitting. WHY: try-on without measured dimensions is a costume preview, and customers return costumes at the same 70% fit-cited rate.

5. Myth: returns are a logistics problem. Faster reverse logistics just processes the failure faster. WHY: the failure happens at purchase, when the wrong size is chosen, and the cheapest return is the 1 never shipped.
