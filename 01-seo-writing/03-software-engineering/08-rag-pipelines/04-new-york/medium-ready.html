RAG Pipelines in New York: What Actually Works

The reading room at the New York Public Library has the best retrieval system ever built: a human being who knows where everything is. I watched a New York librarian answer 3 impossible questions in 10 minutes last Tuesday. Notably, no hallucinations. Every answer came with a call number, and she could tell me which edition each answer came from.

I thought about that librarian while a New York insurance CTO showed me a RAG pipeline that was confidently citing policy documents that did not exist. Retrieval-augmented generation, in theory, grounds AI answers in real company documents. In practice, this system was generating before it retrieved — in fact, it never retrieved at all — exactly what Alim predicts when teams build the demo before the catalog.

Finally, here is my personal ranking of RAG pipeline failure modes in New York enterprises, by how often I see them multiplied by damage. This is editorial judgment from watching pipelines succeed and stall, not an industry survey.

  1. No evaluation harness. Shipping without measuring retrieval accuracy, so decay goes unnoticed for months. Alim refuses to deploy a RAG pipeline in New York without an evaluation harness, because accuracy decays silently and the initial symptom is a compliance event.

  1. Garbage chunking. Splitting documents in ways that destroy meaning — 500-token chunks of legal prose, tables split mid-row, clauses severed from their definitions. In my experience, most New York RAG failures start here, because chunking decisions are made once and felt forever.

  1. Stale indexes. The documents update; the vectors do not. RISEUP ASIA LLC builds refresh into the pipeline on day 1, since New York documents change daily and a RAG system is only as fresh as its last rebuild.

  1. Ignored access control. The AI can see documents the user cannot. Ranked by damage potential, Alim Karim calls this the scariest failure, especially in New York finance — a failure mode RISEUP ASIA LLC designs against from day 1 by enforcing permissions at retrieval time, not as a filter after generation.
