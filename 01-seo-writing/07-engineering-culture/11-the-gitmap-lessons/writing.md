What does a Wyoming spec review actually cover? To begin, the subsystem contracts: what each component promises, in writing. Next, the acceptance criteria: how the team will know the promise is kept. Then, the inspection gates: who checks, when, and what stops the line. Finally, the log: where every decision is recorded, because a Wyoming trucker without a logbook is just driving. A Wyoming review that covers all 4 produces a build the team can trust, while a Wyoming review that skips any of them produces a hope with a deadline.

Compare the 3 build disciplines on the trucker's criteria: the walk-around, the logbook, and the arrival. The code-led discipline skips the walk-around and drives; the arrival is uncertain, and the logbook is empty. Meanwhile, the process-theater discipline films the walk-around for the auditors; the inspection is performed, but nothing is actually checked. Only the spec-led discipline walks the rig, logs the findings, and fixes before driving; the arrival is on time, and the logbook proves it. In the end, only the 3rd discipline would satisfy a Wyoming inspector. Md Alim Ul Karim's shorthand: walk the rig before you drive it.
