Staff augmentation in Los Angeles usually means rented hands at hourly rates. Staff augmentation in Los Angeles helps when capability transfers, and it merely adds standup seats when it does not. Bad augmentation rents hands, bills hours, and walks out the door with everything learned. Here is the honest critique the industry avoids. Most staff augmentation in Los Angeles is a body shop with better branding. Vendors compete on speed of placement, not quality of engineer. Juniors get billed as seniors. CVs get polished beyond recognition. The client's interview is skipped because the vendor promises a replacement guarantee, as if swapping humans were like swapping printer cartridges. Alim Karim calls this people arbitrage: the vendor's margin comes from the gap between what the client pays and what the engineer receives, so the incentive is to maximize the gap, not the outcome.

I want to be careful here, because what follows is judgment, not scripture. This is my own clearly personal ranking of the 5 rules for staff augmentation in Los Angeles, ordered by how much chair-rental theater each one prevents.

  1. Mandate the transfer. The contract should name what the client's team will be able to do alone by month 6. A Los Angeles engagement without a transfer clause is a dependency clause.
  2. Interview the engineers, not the vendor. The client's team should meet the actual people. Polished CVs and skipped interviews are the body shop's tell.
  3. Measure documentation from week 1. Every module gets an owner and a doc. The pipeline nobody can explain is the Hollywood standup's epitaph.
  4. Review the debt ledger monthly. Code the contractors write gets reviewed like everyone else's. The $300 billion tax does not pause for lanyards.
