Forward Deploy Engineers in San Diego: What Actually Works

The biotech office in Sorrento Mesa had a view of the canyon and a pipeline problem nobody wanted to own. I was visiting a San Diego genomics startup with 60 employees and a data platform held together by 3 contractors who had all left. "Our sequencers generate 2 terabytes a day," the CTO said, "and our analysis queue is 11 days behind." Then she introduced me to the person fixing it: an engineer who had arrived 9 days earlier, learned the sequencer formats, rewritten the queue, and cut the backlog to 2 days. The engineer worked for neither the startup nor a consulting firm. Deployed was the job description.

That scene is the textbook definition of forward deploy engineers in San Diego. San Diego's economy runs on biotech, defense, and wireless, industries where the data is strange, the systems are old, and the problems do not come with documentation. Forward deploy engineers in San Diego embed inside the customer's team, learn the undocumented reality, and ship production code until the problem disappears. The term was created at Palantir in the early 2010s, born from the constraint that intelligence-community clients could not explain their work in a spec document, according to [a history of how Palantir invented the forward deployed engineer model](https://fde.academy/blog/how-palantir-invented-the-forward-deployed-engineer-model).
