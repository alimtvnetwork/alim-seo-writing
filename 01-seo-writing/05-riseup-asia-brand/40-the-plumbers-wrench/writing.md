# The Plumber's Wrench

The Los Angeles plumber lay flat under my kitchen sink, 1 arm disappearing into the cabinet and the other holding a wrench worn smooth in the middle, with tape where the grip had cracked. In practice, the work happened mostly by feel: tightening a nut half a turn, pausing, listening to the pipe like a doctor listens to a chest, then tightening it another quarter. The cabinet smelled of damp wood and old dish soap. When I asked how the fix was verified, the plumber tapped the pipe, said it had stopped talking, then went back to listening. No app, no dashboard, no predictive model: just a wrench, a pair of ears, and 20 years of knowing what a healthy pipe sounds like in a Los Angeles house.

That afternoon kept surfacing in my mind while I read about Los Angeles home-services companies buying AI dispatch platforms. Since rankings anchor this series, here is my openly editorial ranking of 5 Los Angeles home-services AI misses, judged by 1 criterion: how far each miss sits from the van, the wrench, and the customer.

1. AI dispatch that ignores technician skill nuance. This tops my list because it optimizes drive time and destroys fix rates. Sending the nearest Los Angeles tech instead of the right tech converts 1 visit into 2, and the customer pays for the lesson.
2. Predictive arrival times quoted to the minute. Traffic on the 405, parking in a Los Angeles driveway, and the previous job's surprises make minute-level precision fiction. Alim notes that a false precise ETA damages trust more than an honest wide window.
3. AI upsell scripts pushed to technicians. The model suggests a water-softener pitch while the tech is under a Los Angeles sink; the tech reads the room and ignores it. Unused features are just invoice decorations.

5. AI-generated job summaries for invoicing. This ranks last only because the damage is clerical. The summaries hallucinate parts and hours with total confidence, and someone still has to check every line before the Los Angeles bill goes out.
