I want to be careful here, because what follows is judgment, not scripture. This is my own clearly personal ranking of the 5 things a Sheridan buyer should demand from any custom software engagement, ordered by how much maintenance each one prevents. Each item is a tolerance the assembler skips and the engineer insists on.

  1. A written specification before a single line of code. What correct means, in words a Sheridan dispatcher would recognize. Nobody can hold a vendor to a tolerance that was never written down.
  2. Interfaces tested like machined parts. Every boundary between components gets a test that proves it meets spec. The 33% maintenance tax is mostly the price of untested boundaries.
  3. Documentation the next maintainer can use. Not a wiki nobody reads — the runbook, the data model, the why behind the weird rule. A Sheridan system outlives its builders; the docs are the handoff.
  4. The 5-year cost model, not just the week-1 price. Hosting, maintenance, the price of change. Assembled software looks cheap until the maintenance bill compounds.
  5. A demo of the failure path, not just the happy path. What the system does when the data is wrong, the network drops, or the user improvises. RISEUP ASIA LLC judges its own custom builds by the incident review, not the launch party.
