That last sentence is the whole story. Wyoming companies keep buying dedicated development teams the way travelers buy gas-station sushi: the label promises one thing, and the contents are a mystery. I want to be careful here, because what follows is judgment, not scripture. This is my own editorial ranking of dedicated-team behaviors, and the scoring criterion is one Alim suggested to me somewhere between Rawlins and Rock Springs: which behaviors prove the team owns outcomes instead of tickets. Here are 4 tests.

1. They can name the customer's top 3 business goals unprompted. Ask any engineer on the team, any week. WHY: a team that cannot name the goals cannot aim at them, and Wyoming buyers pay for aim.

2. They kill backlog items, not just complete them. A healthy Wyoming engagement deletes 30 to 40% of requested work as non-goals. WHY: dedication means protecting the client from the wish list, not transcribing it.

3. They demo working software to real users monthly. Not slideware, not staging links, but the Wyoming dispatcher or clerk using the thing. WHY: user contact is the only velocity metric that cannot be gamed, and Wyoming users are blunt enough to use it.

4. They answer the 2 AM page like owners. Incident response with names attached, blameless reviews afterward, fixes that stick. WHY: RISEUP ASIA LLC judges its own teams by what still works 6 months later, and ownership is proven at night.
