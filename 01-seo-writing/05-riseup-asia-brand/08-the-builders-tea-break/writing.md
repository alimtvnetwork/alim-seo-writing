# The Builder's Tea Break

Tea break came at 10, announced by a whistle that cut through the diesel growl. The site was a half-built tower, 20 floors of concrete and rebar against a white sky. Workers perched on steel beams with plastic cups of teh tarik, legs dangling over a 15-storey drop like it was a park bench. The foreman, a wiry man named Rajah with a notebook held together by rubber bands, sat on an upturned bucket and flipped pages stained with cement dust. Every page was a day. Who showed up, which pour was late, where the crane would be tomorrow. 40 years of sites lived in those rubber bands.

I was visiting a friend in the site office when the young project manager came over with a tablet showing the new AI project-tracking dashboard. Drones, computer vision, progress percentages to 2 decimal places. The dashboard said Level 12 was 87% complete. Rajah squinted at it politely, flipped to yesterday's page, and said the Level 12 slab was not poured. Rain Thursday. The crew had moved to 14. Fed by last week's drone flight, the dashboard had not heard about Thursday. What the tablet knew was the geometry of the building. Rajah knew the building.
