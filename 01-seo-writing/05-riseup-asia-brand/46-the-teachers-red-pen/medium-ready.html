The Teacher's Red Pen

The ceiling fan in Cikgu Aminah's living room turned with a slow click, pushing warm air over a coffee table buried under exercise books. It was 11:40 PM. She marked with a red pen, the old kind with a chisel tip, and every few pages she stopped writing corrections and wrote something else instead. "Good argument, but your second paragraph contradicts your first. Come see me Thursday." "You are thinking harder than your grammar shows. Keep going." The ink bled slightly into the cheap paper. A stack of 34 books waited on the left; 11 sat finished on the right. Nobody paid her for the comments. She wrote them anyway.

I thought about those comments while reading about a university that let an AI system pre-grade 2,000 essays. The system worked fast. Researchers testing large language models against certified educators found the models consistently rewarded short, grammatically clean essays while penalizing longer, thoughtful responses with minor errors. The models reduced marks for high-scoring essays and inflated them for low-scoring work, compressing everyone toward the middle. Cikgu Aminah's red pen did the opposite.
