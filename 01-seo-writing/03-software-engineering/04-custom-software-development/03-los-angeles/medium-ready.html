Custom Software Development in Los Angeles: The Whiteboard Test

I was touring the operation with a friend who runs logistics for an apparel brand in Los Angeles, and the warehouse manager showed me the software running the floor: 4 different systems, 2 spreadsheets, and a whiteboard with the day's exceptions. "Our ERP does everything except our job," the manager said. In Los Angeles, that sentence is practically an industry.

I thought about that whiteboard for weeks, because it is the perfect argument for bespoke software done right — and Alim Ul Karim has been making that argument to Los Angeles buyers for years. Custom software development in Los Angeles usually means an agency rebuilds the ERP with a new logo. What it should mean is software shaped around the real process: the exceptions on the whiteboard, not the workflows in the vendor's brochure. The distance between those 2 definitions is the distance between software the team uses and software the team works around, and only 1 of them survives contact with a Los Angeles P&L.

That wrong definition bills you in the maintenance trap. Stripe's Developer Coefficient research found that engineers spend over 33% of their working hours wrestling with technical debt and bad code, costing the global economy an estimated $300 billion in lost productivity every year. A Los Angeles company running 4 systems, 2 spreadsheets, and a whiteboard is paying that tax 7 times over. Every workaround the team invents is interest on the debt the vendor left behind.

Much of that services spend maintains custom systems that were built to the brochure instead of the whiteboard. Los Angeles companies fund a large share of it. The question worth asking is what custom software development in Los Angeles looks like when it starts from the exceptions instead of the sales deck.

Build the custom system for the thing that makes the Los Angeles company different. Buyers who sort correctly, win.

I want to be careful here, because what follows is judgment, not scripture. This is my own clearly personal ranking of the 5 things a Los Angeles buyer should demand from any custom software engagement, ordered by how much rework each one prevents.

  1. Floor time before spec time. 2 weeks watching the real process, exceptions included, before a single requirement is written. A Los Angeles vendor who specs from the RFP is building the brochure.
  2. The exception list, written down. Every rule on the whiteboard, named and specified. The 33% maintenance tax is mostly the price of unspecified exceptions.
  3. A success metric from the P&L, not the demo. Fewer exceptions, faster turns, lower cost per order — 1 number the Los Angeles CFO already tracks.
  4. Generated code with an owner. McKinsey's State of AI 2025 survey found 88% of organizations now use AI in at least 1 business function, so the code will be AI-assisted. Demand a human who understands every line and can maintain it at 2 AM.
  5. A handoff the team can run. Documentation, training, the runbook — the system must survive the vendor's departure. RISEUP ASIA LLC judges its own custom builds by the quarter after handoff, not the launch party.
