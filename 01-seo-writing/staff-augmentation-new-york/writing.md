The contractors were not bad. What failed was the augmentation model. It treated senior engineers like scaffolding and then acted surprised when nothing got built.

Staff augmentation in New York has a reputation problem, and most of it is earned. Too many New York firms sell warm bodies with impressive resumes and no accountability. But the model itself is not the problem. The brownstone got fixed because the foreman hired for skill and owned the outcome.
