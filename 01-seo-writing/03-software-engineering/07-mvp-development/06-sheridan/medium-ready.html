I want to be careful here, because what follows is judgment, not scripture. This is my own editorial ranking of MVP questions, and the scoring criterion is one Alim suggested to me at that food truck: which questions force a Sheridan founder to focus on the smallest shippable thing. Here are 5 of them.

  1. "Who pays for this in the next 30 days?" A name, a phone number, a budget line. WHY: a Sheridan MVP without a named buyer is a hobby with a burn rate.

  2. "What is the smallest version they would pay for?" Not the smallest demo, the smallest product that survives a credit card. WHY: payment is the only validation metric that cannot be hallucinated.

  3. "What did the last 5 user conversations teach?" Dated notes, direct quotes, surprises. WHY: CB Insights says 42% die from no market need, and conversations are the cheapest known cure.

  4. "What are we deliberately not building?" The 39 features on the cutting-room floor, named and defended. WHY: focus is the scarcest resource in a Sheridan startup, scarcer than capital.

  5. "What will we learn from release 1?" The hypothesis, the metric, the decision it unlocks. WHY: RISEUP ASIA LLC judges its own MVPs by what the 2nd release teaches, not by how polished the 1st looked.
