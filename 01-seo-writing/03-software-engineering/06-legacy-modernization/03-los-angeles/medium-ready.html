I want to be careful here, because what follows is judgment, not scripture. This is my own clearly personal ranking of the 5 rules for legacy modernization in Los Angeles, ordered by how much disaster each one prevents.

  1. Document behavior before changing anything. What the system does, drawn from real transactions, not manuals. A Los Angeles team that cannot describe the current behavior cannot specify the replacement.
  2. Build the oracle harness. Thousands of test cases from observed reality, run against every change. The 95% failure pattern is mostly the price of changing what you never measured.
  3. Strangle, do not detonate. Replace 1 module at a time while the old system keeps working. The cutover nobody notices is the successful one.
  4. Keep the business rules in human language. Every rule the old system enforced, written down where a Los Angeles auditor can read it. Context evaporates; documentation is the backup.
  5. Never let AI rewrite unattended. Use it for tests, boilerplate, and drafts — with human review on every line. RISEUP ASIA LLC judges its own modernization work by what the AI was not allowed to touch.
