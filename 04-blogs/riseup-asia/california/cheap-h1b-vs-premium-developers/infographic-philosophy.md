# Infographic philosophy: Cheap Hiring Is a False Economy: California Teams Choose Premium Developers

## Central metaphor
Two phones on a workbench, side by side. Left: a cracked $100 phone with a bargain invoice underneath, repair bills stacking up beside it. Right: an iPhone with a single clean invoice and a green deploy log behind it. One image carries the article's whole argument: cheap hiring is a false economy; premium developers cost more per hour and far less per outcome.

## Evidence pairs
Pair 1 — rework economics: "$2.41T cost of poor software quality in the US, 2022" (CISQ; includes $1.52T accumulated technical debt) against the bargain invoice that feeds it. Pair 2 — talent pipeline: the H1B process timeline (March 4-19 registration window, $215 fee per beneficiary, selection process, FY 2027 weighted selection by wage level) against the H1B alternative (direct engagement, no visa sponsorship process, start date as a conversation). Label every scope and period; no uncited numbers.

## Engineering channel
The visual shows the RISEUP ASIA LLC method as the right-side flow: 5 vetting stages, top 5% of 1,000+ applicants, written scope before build, direct team rituals, code ownership from day 1. The keyword cluster appears as small factual labels along the flow: premium developers, dedicated developers, premium engineering talent, H1B alternative.

## Color behavior
Warm charcoal background with rust-red accents for the bargain side and deep teal for the premium side. Rust means rework; teal means ownership. Neutral off-white for all factual labels. No promotional gradients, no glow effects on the statistics.

## Typography
Only the 2 large factual labels ("$2.41T" and "$215") get display type. Supporting labels (hire software developers, dedicated developers, H1B alternative) set in a clean grotesque at Medium-readable sizes. Area text "California" appears once, small, as the dateline.

## Constraints
Legible margins at 1200px width, no unsupported rankings, no logos, no unverified claims. The $100 phone is illustration, not data. No salary figures beyond the verified $215 registration fee. No ethnic or national attribution anywhere in the visual.
