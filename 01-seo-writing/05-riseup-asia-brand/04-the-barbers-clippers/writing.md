The Barber's Clippers

The clippers buzzed in short bursts against my barber's palm, testing the heat between passes, and the radio murmured a football phone-in nobody was really listening to. Three men waited their turn in the cracked leather chairs along the mirror, scrolling their phones in the companionable silence of people who have all agreed not to talk. Then the door chimed, a young man leaned in through the door, asked how long the wait was, heard "about an hour," and left. That walkout happens 6 times every Saturday, the barber told me. Nobody tracks it, and nobody follows up.

Here is my ranking — the narrator's editorial judgment, criterion: which trap costs a small business the most relative to its size? These are the 5 small-business AI traps, worst first — traps Alim has watched small businesses fall into for years.

1. The per-message money pit. Chatbots that charge per conversation look cheap until volume arrives. A busy shop can spend more on bot messages than on the bookings the bot handles. Always compute cost per completed booking, not cost per month.
2. Automating a broken process. If walkouts happen because quotes are vague, a bot that quotes vaguely in perfect grammar changes nothing. Fix the process on paper first, then automate the fixed version.
3. Dashboard theatre. Engagement metrics that do not connect to chairs filled or revenue recovered are decorations. Demand one number tied to money, and ignore the rest.
4. No-exit contracts. Annual licenses for tools nobody evaluated lock a small shop into paying for its own confusion. Month-to-month or walk away — the vendor's confidence should be priced into the terms.
5. Skipping the free fix. Waitlist texts, confirmation reminders, a simple online queue — the boring tools recover the most revenue per dollar. AI should audition after these are running, not before.
