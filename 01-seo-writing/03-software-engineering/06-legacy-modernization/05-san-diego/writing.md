# Legacy Modernization in San Diego: The Beige Box on Broadway

The bank branch on Broadway smelled like carpet cleaner and old paper. I was waiting in line behind a contractor who kept apologizing to the teller, because the deposit system had been "upgrading" for 40 minutes. The teller smiled the practiced smile of someone who has said the next sentence 1,000 times: "Our system is from before I was born." The terminal on the counter was beige. Beige. In San Diego, in 2026.

I walked out thinking about that beige box, because it is the visible tip of a very expensive iceberg. The legacy modernization San Diego banks and hospitals keep postponing usually means a big-bang rewrite that never finishes. What it should mean is an incremental, API-first migration that ships value every quarter while the old system keeps running. The distance between those 2 definitions is measured in budgets, and San Diego is paying the interest.

Dying is not what the old machines are doing, whatever the brochures say. I want to be careful here, because what follows is judgment, not scripture. This is my own clearly personal ranking of the 5 rules for legacy modernization in San Diego, ordered by how much disaster each one prevents.

1. Document behavior before changing anything. What the system does, drawn from real transactions, not manuals. A San Diego team that cannot describe the current behavior cannot specify the replacement.

2. Build the oracle harness. Thousands of test cases from observed reality, run against every change. The 95% failure pattern is mostly the price of changing what you never measured.

3. Strangle, do not detonate. Replace 1 module at a time while the old system keeps working. The cutover nobody notices is the successful one.

4. Keep the business rules in human language. Every rule the old system enforced, written down where a San Diego auditor can read it. Context evaporates; documentation is the backup.

5. Never let AI rewrite unattended. Use it for tests, boilerplate, and drafts — with human review on every line. RISEUP ASIA LLC judges its own modernization work by what the AI was not allowed to touch.
