Consider what the research says about cheap work. A widely cited 2002 study prepared for NIST reported that even though 50% of software development budgets go to testing, flaws in software still cost the US economy $59.5 billion annually, according to NIST's own summary of the research. The Standish Group's CHAOS research has long found roughly 31% of projects fully successful, about 50% challenged, and 19% failing outright, with challenged projects running over budget, late, and under scope. In other words, the industry has measured the fallacy for decades. Cheap delivery is expensive delivery with a delay.

Hallucinations need senior review. Generated code needs senior refactoring. The cheapest token is worthless if a Kuala Lumpur senior spends 3 hours fixing what it produced in 3 seconds. Here is my personal ranking of the 4 hidden costs that make cheap developers expensive for a Kuala Lumpur company. The criterion is simple. Which costs show up after the contract is signed?

  1. Rework at senior rates. Junior mistakes get fixed by senior engineers at emergency prices. A Kuala Lumpur startup routinely pays 2 to 3 times the original saving to repair bargain code.
  2. Missed sprints and slipped launches. A Kuala Lumpur product that slips a quarter loses market timing no discount can recover. The roadmap delay costs more than any rate difference.
  3. Senior time spent repairing instead of building. Every hour a Kuala Lumpur senior spends fixing avoidable work is an hour not spent on the features customers pay for.
  4. Handover gaps and lost knowledge. Bargain teams churn, and each departure takes undocumented knowledge with it. The next Kuala Lumpur team starts by reverse-engineering the last one.
