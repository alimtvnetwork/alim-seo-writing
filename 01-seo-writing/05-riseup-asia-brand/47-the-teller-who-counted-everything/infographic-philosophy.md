# Infographic Philosophy — The Teller Who Counted Everything

The story's spine is a contrast between a teller who verifies every number and a chatbot that verifies nothing. The infographics make that contrast numerical and visual.

## Infographic 1 — the stat card (57%)

The Saturn fintech finding dominates: 57% of AI answers to real financial questions were wrong, rising to 88% on harder questions. One number, enormous, because the story's critique of banking AI stands or falls on measured accuracy rather than vibes. The source line names the study's scale (18 models, 121 questions, 10,000+ answers) so the figure reads as evidence, not decoration.

## Infographic 2 — the compare card

Red column (THE AI-SPENDING PATTERN) versus green column (THE ENGINEER-LED PATTERN), each row pairing a failure with its engineered replacement: guessing vs grounded retrieval, no evaluation vs scored on the 200 hardest questions, per-token billing vs cost per resolved query, no owner vs named owner. The mirror structure echoes the story's side-by-side section and lets a skimmer absorb the whole argument in seconds.

## Infographic 3 — the ranking card

The 4 ways banks waste AI budgets, with the editorial criterion stated plainly at the top (money burned per unit of return). Each entry keeps its WHY compressed to a single line — "uncited answers become fines" carries the regulatory sting of the story's first entry. Navy badges preserve the 1-4 order from the article.

Same 1200x630 canvas, navy header band, system sans-serif, and the "RISEUP ASIA LLC · story series" footer used across the series.
