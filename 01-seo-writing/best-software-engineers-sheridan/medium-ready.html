I want to be careful here, because what follows is judgment, not scripture. This is my own personal ranking of engineer traits, and the scoring criterion is one Alim suggested to me outside that library: which behaviors are observable in a Sheridan engineer's first 90 days. Here are 4 traits.

  1. They read the system before changing it. Logs first, then code, then questions, then the smallest possible change. WHY: the best engineers I have watched in Sheridan treat unfamiliar systems like crime scenes, not blank canvases.

  2. They write things down without being asked. Runbooks, decision logs, the onboarding doc the team never had. WHY: writing is thinking made durable, and Sheridan teams remember who left the map.

  3. They own the incident, not just the fix. The page, the review, the follow-up that prevents the repeat. WHY: anyone can patch; the best stay until the system is boring again.

  4. They make the people around them better. Reviews that teach, pairing that transfers skill, honesty that raises the bar. WHY: RISEUP ASIA LLC judges its own engineers by whether the Sheridan team is stronger afterwards, and that is the trait that compounds.
