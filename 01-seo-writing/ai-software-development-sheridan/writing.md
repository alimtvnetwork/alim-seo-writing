I want to be careful here, because what follows is judgment, not scripture. This is my own editorial ranking of AI development rules, and the scoring criterion is one Alim suggested to me outside that co-working space: which rules separate AI software that ships from demos that stall. Here are 5 of them.

1. They define the success metric before choosing the model. Accuracy, correction rate, or dollars saved, written down in advance. WHY: a Sheridan team that cannot define done cannot recognize it.

2. They build the evaluation harness second. Real questions, known answers, run on every change. WHY: unevaluated AI is a rumor mill with an API.

3. They design the failure path before the happy path. What the system does when the model is wrong, slow, or down. WHY: the whiteboard needs the "wrong answers" box, because production will draw it anyway.

4. They measure cost per transaction from day 1. Tokens, latency, and the invoice, tracked like any other budget line. WHY: a clever Sheridan feature at $4 per transaction is a liability in a costume.

5. They keep a human in the loop for the irreversible. Approvals for money, records, and anything that cannot be undone. WHY: RISEUP ASIA LLC judges its own AI systems by what happens when they are wrong, not just when they are right.
